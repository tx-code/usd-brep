# Known issues

Known bugs in the SMLib geometry SDK.

## Modeling

### `heal_brep` can report success when a repair failed

Failed seam movement/insertion and failed recursive property refresh now propagate through
`heal_brep` (`SmApiHealBrep`). Other helpers still discard failures, including degenerate-face
repair, seam-edge splitting and sheet/region repair. The native optional UV-trim creation stage
also warns and returns success when an individual face fails. These remaining paths need their
own reproductions and regressions; success does not establish complete repair.

**Workaround:** check the healed Brep with `is_manifold_solid()` and, in development builds, `_smlib_dev.assert_valid()`. These checks cover their respective topology contracts; they do not establish complete geometric validity.

### Healing closed surfaces with missing or coincident seams

`heal_brep`, and the healing run on import, can mis-repair faces on periodic and closed surfaces such as tori, spheres and cylinders. These faces mostly come from other CAD systems.

- Directly inserting the first seam on an uncut torus can still split it into two faces when the seam is an independent 3D circle or B-spline curve. The fix supports curves defined on the surface (`SmCrvOnSurf`), including seams generated by the healer.
- A sphere bounded by two coincident seam edges is treated as collapsed and deleted.
- A torus whose one seam edge is used by two loops is treated as collapsed.
- A face with edge loops and a missing seam can be skipped by seam insertion, because its loops are classified too early.
- An edge-to-face gap near a seam can be measured on the wrong side of the surface, so healing tightens the edge tolerance too far.
- A face in which one degenerate edge is used by two of its loops is left with invalid topology.
- Pole-crossing detection can disagree with the edge splitter near an endpoint: the detector finds an interior parameter, but the splitter rejects it using the endpoint tolerance. Healing reports the face, edge, parameter, interval, and endpoint tolerance, then skips that face/edge/parameter candidate for the rest of the repair invocation and continues with other candidates, including other pole crossings on a shared edge. The defect stays flagged; this disagreement still needs investigation.

**Workaround:** none. Check healed Breps as described above.

### `drop_curve_to_surface` near a surface pole

Near a surface pole, for example the apex of a cone, dropping a curve onto a surface can miss a seam that the curve starts on or runs along within tolerance, and so classify the curve's boundary incorrectly.

### `shell_brep` can return a result that is not a solid

Shelling `TestFiles/pt_TestFiles/Shell/problemShell4.smb` outward by 5.08 with face 2 open returns three faces that do not form a solid. The debug `ShellBrep` test for this file expects a placeholder face count of `9999`. With `extended_offset=False`, shelling a 50 x 50 x 30 box whose top edges are filleted, with its top face open and an inset of 2, returns 83 faces that do not form a solid.

**Workaround:** for the filleted box, use the default `extended_offset=True`.

## Measurement

### `brep_relationship` can report a shallow overlap as touching

`brep_relationship` (`SmApiBrepRelationship`) looks for overlaps at sample points: vertices, a point on each face, and points along edges. An overlap that contains none of them is reported as `touching`, not `interpenetrating`. For example, a radius-1 sphere sunk 0.2 into the `x = 20` face of `create_box((0, 0, 0), 20, 20, 10)` is `touching`.

## Tessellation

### Inside-out triangles next to fillets

On solids filleted with `circular_fillet`, `tessellate()` can wind some triangles against their face's normal, so they render inside out. Seen on cylinder caps and on cones next to fillets.

**Workaround:** a `surface_angle_tolerance_deg` of 15° avoided every case found.

### Long thin triangles at sphere poles

For some chord heights, `tessellate()` on a sphere builds long, thin triangle fans at both poles, beside the seam, that stray far beyond the chord height. This happens when the chord height is between about 1.4e-4 and 3.2e-4 times the radius. For a radius-5 sphere, a chord height of 0.001 leaves a triangle 0.066 off the surface, and 0.0016 leaves one 0.72 off. The angle tolerances make no difference. ([GitHub #7][gh-7])

**Workaround:** use a chord height of at most 1.2e-4 or at least 3.6e-4 times the radius.

### Skinned faces exceed the chord height

`tessellate()` does not keep a skinned face within `chord_height_tolerance` when the skin (`create_skin_primitive`) has more than two sections. On a 20 x 20 square skinned through four sections twisted 10° over 100, triangles lie 0.20 off the surface at a chord height of 0.05, and 0.024 off at 0.001. The angle tolerances make no difference to skinned faces: at a chord height of 0 and 1° angle tolerances, each side face is two triangles. A two-section skin meets the chord height. ([GitHub #20][gh-20])

**Workaround:** set `max_edge_length`. At 5 on the skin above, triangles lie within 0.004 of the surface.
